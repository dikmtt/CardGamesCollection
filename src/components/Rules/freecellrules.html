Overview

FreeCell is invented by Paul Alfille in the 1970s, first introduced to PLATO computing systems in 1978. It became one of the most popular solitaire games included in Microsoft systems, and the second one to do so after Klondike.

Basics

The object of the game is to play all cards to the foundations. Each foundation pile starts with Ace and builds up in suit to King. There are 8 tableau piles that build down in alternate colors from King to Ace. There are four free cells that can only hold one card each.

Setup

The tableau consists of 8 columns. The first 4 columns have 7 cards, and the last 4 have 6.

The Play

To move a card between tableau spaces, the card must be one value lower than the target column's top card and of a different color. You can move stacks, but the size is limited by the amount of free cells available. (General formula: number of available free cells + 1) Some examples:

  1. A 2 of Clubs can be moved to a 3 of Diamonds.
  2. A 6 of Hearts cannot be moved to a 7 of Hearts. The colors must alternate.
  3. If 2 of 4 free cells are occupied, you can move a valid run of at most 3 cards.
There are 4 free cells. You can move any card to a free cell, but they can only occupy 1 card each. Cards may also be moved from the free cells to either the tableau or foundations. Empty spaces in the tableau can be filled with any card or valid run.

If an available Ace is exposed, you can move it to a foundation pile. Then you can start building up in suit by moving a valid card to the foundations. Once all cards are moved to the foundations, you win the game.

Interactions

  • Click on a card or stack to select it. Click on a different card to move it. If it is a valid play, the card or stack will move to the target column.
  • The free cells have yellow borders at the start. The foundations have white borders.
  • The game allows 1-click movement to the foundations after a moment in which all tableau columns are exactly descending in rank.
  • You can choose between 3 deal types: A completely random shuffle, or one of Microsoft's original 32,000 deals either by random or from typing a seed number.

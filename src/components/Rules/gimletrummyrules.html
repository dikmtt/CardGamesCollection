Overview

Gimlet Rummy is a solitaire version of Gin Rummy created by John Burton and showcased by Solitaire With the Card Guy in his YouTube channel. The object of the game is go to "gimlet" (end with no deadwood) before one discard pile ends up with six cards.

Basics

The object of Gin Rummy is to meld your cards into sets (3 or 4 cards of the same rank) or runs (3 or more cards of the same suit in sequential order) and minimizing your deadwood (cards not a part of a run or set). Each card has a point amount: Aces 1, numbered cards their face value, and face cards 10. A player needs to have at most 10 deadwood points in order to be able to "go out".

Setup

Deal a hand of 10 cards. The rest will act as the stock pile.

The Play

Take the top card of the stock pile. If you want the card, put it in your hand and discard a different card from your hand. If not, then it is immediately put to the discard pile. There are 4 discard piles, one for each suit. Discard the card to its suit's respective discard pile. A discard pile may not have more than 6 cards. If one of those ever has 6 cards or more, the game ends immediately and you score your deadwood points.

Ideally, you want cards that improve your hand and want to discard as many deadwood points as possible, because the object is to bring it down to zero. A card may only be part of one meld. Aces are always low.

The Endgame

If all your cards are part of a meld, you "go gimlet" and win. If scoring is available, then your score is determined by the size of each discard pile plus 20 points for "going gimlet." Otherwise, your hand has "gone sour" and the score is your deadwood points plus 30 for your "imaginary opponent." A scoring game plays to 100, at that point the party that scores it first wins the entire game.

Interactions

  • Click on on the stock pile to draw a card. If you want it in your hand, click on the newly drawn card. Then click on a card from your hand to discard it, and that card immediately goes to its respective suit's discard pile. The game keeps track of the number of cards on each discard pile.
  • If you wish to pass on the card, click on the stock pile to draw another card. The old card is instantly put into the suit's discard pile.
  • Play continues until one pile reaches 6 cards. If that happens, or you "go gimlet", the game ends and your deadwood score is calculated.

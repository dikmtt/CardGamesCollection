Overview

Hearts is a trick-taking card game. The objective of the game is to be the player with the least amount of points when the game ends, usually when a player reaches a certain target score. In most digital implementations, including this one, the Black Lady variant is used, where the Queen of Spades is worth 13 points. It's also the version that Microsoft uses in its Hearts programs from Windows 95 to 7.

Basics

In trick-taking games, each player plays a card, and after every player plays, the cards form a "trick". The player who played the highest card according to game rules wins all cards in the trick. In Hearts, the number of tricks taken doesn't matter, what matters is the cards captured in each trick. Each heart card is worth 1 penalty point, and the Queen of Spades is worth 13. You want to end up with the lowest score at the end of the game, so it's typically best to avoid tricks containing them.

Setup

Hearts is most commonly played with 4 players, as seen in both this implementation as well as most implementations online. However, by removing certain cards, the game can be played with 3 or 5 players. In each hand, players are dealt evenly - 13 cards for each player in a 4-player game.

Passing Cards

In both this version and most implementations of Hearts, players pass 3 cards to another player before playing cards, and the passing rotation changes each round, looping between Left, Right, Across, and Keep. Left and Right mean players pass 3 cards to the player on the left and right of them respectively, and Across means players pass cards to the players opposite from them. Every fourth round, there is no passing, and the passing rotation returns back to Left after the round finishes.

The Play

Each round starts with the 2 of Clubs. Players must play a card of the same suit as the trick's first card if possible (following suit). If not, they may play any card. Players cannot start a trick (leading) with a heart unless hearts have been broken. There are two ways to do so:

  1. A player is unable to follow suit and plays a heart card
  2. It is a player's turn to lead and their only cards in their hand are hearts
A trick is won by the player who played the highest card in the lead card's suit. There is never a trump suit in Hearts. The player who won the trick leads the next trick.

Scoring

After all tricks in a round have been played and all players' hands are empty, each player counts the number of penalty points captured in tricks, scoring 1 for each heart and 13 for the Q-S. The game continues with a new rounds until a player reaches the minimum target score, such as 50 or 100, in which the player with the least amount of points wins.

Shooting the moon

Modern implementations of Hearts include a special rule where if a player captures all penalty cards in one round, they don't score 26 points but instead give it to all other players, or deduct 26 from their score. This rule, known as "Shooting the Moon", is also implemented here, and is the only time in which taking many points (if not all of them) are beneficial.

Miscellaneous

  • The Queen of Spades in this variant does not break hearts, but it can be led anytime.
  • Players cannot dump penalty cards (Hearts or the Q-S) in the first trick of each round. If they can't follow suit, they must play a spade or diamond.

Interactions

  • Click on a card to play it to a trick. The game will tell you if the play is valid or not.
  • During the card passing phase, click on a card to highlight it, indicating you want to pass that card. Once three cards have been highlighted, click the PASS button to start passing. You are given some time to analyze which cards have been passed back to you, before each round starts.

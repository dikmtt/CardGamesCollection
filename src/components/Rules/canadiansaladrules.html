Overview

Canadian Salad is a trick-taking card game. The objective of the game is to be the player with the least amount of points after 6 rounds. Each round, the penalty rules change.

Basics

In trick-taking games, each player plays a card, and after every player plays the cards form a "trick". The player who played the highest card according to game rules wins all cards in the trick. In Canadian Salad, taking tricks that contain penalty cards is bad, so it's typically best to avoid those tricks.

Setup

Canadian Salad is most commonly played with 4 players, but by removing certain cards, the game can be played with 3 or players. In each hand, players are dealt evenly - 13 cards for each player in a 4-player game.

The 6 Rounds

Canadian Salad contains 6 rounds, each with a different way of taking penalty points.

  1. No Tricks: Each trick taken scores 10 penalty points
  2. No Hearts: Each heart card captured in tricks scores 10 penalty points
  3. No Queens: Each queen card captured in tricks scores 25 penalty points
  4. No Spade King: The King of Spades is worth 100 penalty points
  5. No Last Trick: The player who won the last trick scores 100 penalty points
  6. Salad: All penalty rules from Rounds 1 to 5 apply.

The Play

Players must play a card of the same suit as the trick's first card if possible (following suit). If not, they may play any card. A trick is won by the player who played the highest card in the lead card's suit. There is never a trump suit in Canadian Salad.

Scoring

After all tricks in a round have been played and all players' hands are empty, each player counts the number of penalty points captured in or from tricks, scoring points as stated by the rules The game continues with a new rounds until all 6 rounds have been played, in which the player with the least amount of points wins.
